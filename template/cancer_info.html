🧬 Cancer Information Center

What is Cancer?

Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow and divide uncontrollably. Normally, cells in the human body grow, divide and die in a controlled manner. Cancer occurs when this control mechanism fails.

These abnormal cells can form masses called tumors and may invade nearby tissues. In some cases, cancer cells can spread to other parts of the body through the blood or lymphatic system, a process known as metastasis.

There are more than 100 different types of cancer, affecting various organs and tissues including the breast, lungs, skin, blood, brain and digestive system.

How Cancer Develops

Cancer begins when genetic changes occur inside a cell. These changes may be inherited from parents or acquired during a person's lifetime due to environmental factors, radiation exposure, infections or random errors during cell division.

The damaged genes can affect the normal regulation of cell growth. Instead of stopping growth when necessary, abnormal cells continue dividing and multiplying.

Over time, these abnormal cells accumulate and form tumors. Some tumors remain localized, while others become malignant and spread to nearby tissues and distant organs.

Scientists continue to study the genetic and molecular mechanisms behind cancer development to improve prevention, diagnosis and treatment methods.
